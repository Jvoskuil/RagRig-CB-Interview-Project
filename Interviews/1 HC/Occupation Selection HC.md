## 1. Domain interpretation

**Domain definition:** Healthcare encompasses all occupations involved in promoting, maintaining, or restoring health through direct patient care, technical support, administration, research, public health, and allied health professions. [cpd.emory](https://cpd.emory.edu/channels/health/)

**Included occupations:** Clinical roles (physicians, nurses, therapists, technicians), allied health professions (radiologic technologists, dietitians, respiratory therapists), technical roles (laboratory, imaging, biomedical equipment), administrative roles (health information management, compliance, revenue cycle), public health and research roles (epidemiologists, clinical research coordinators), and support roles with meaningful decision-making (pharmacists, social workers, patient navigators). [cpd.emory](https://cpd.emory.edu/channels/health/)

**Excluded occupations:** Purely clerical roles without meaningful cognitive decision episodes (medical receptionists, orderlies, transport aides), environmental services without clinical judgment (housekeeping, food service), and roles that are essentially identical to already-used occupations (Emergency Department Attending Physician, Attending Physician in Hospital Internal Medicine Service). [coursera](https://www.coursera.org/articles/job-in-the-medical-field)

**Geographic and industry assumptions:** Broadly transferable across jurisdictions with minimal country-specific regulatory dependence. Includes hospital-based, outpatient, community, laboratory, and administrative settings. Mixed industry scope: acute care, primary care, public health, research, and health information management. [cpd.emory](https://cpd.emory.edu/channels/health/)

**Important ambiguity:** Some roles (e.g., Nurse Practitioner, Physician Assistant) may have jurisdiction-specific scope-of-practice variations. These are marked as regionally adaptable. Occupational titles follow internationally recognizable conventions where possible. [shawnee](https://www.shawnee.edu/blog/clinical-administrative-careers-in-health-science)

## 3. Detailed occupation profiles

### Occupation H01: Clinical Pharmacist (Hospital Inpatient)

- **Standard title:** Clinical Pharmacist (Inpatient/Hospital)
- **Role classification:** Recognized specialization within pharmacy practice
- **Domain fit:** Directly involved in medication therapy management, drug–drug interaction management, and prescribing decisions in acute care. [bmjopen.bmj](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/13/12/e075512)
- **Typical work setting:** Hospital inpatient units, pharmacy department, interdisciplinary rounds
- **Expertise level:** Independent to senior practitioner (PharmD + residency/board certification)
- **Main stakeholders:** Physicians, nurses, patients, pharmacy technicians, hospital administration
- **Core tasks:**
  - Review medication orders for appropriateness, interactions, and dosing.
  - Participate in interdisciplinary rounds to optimize pharmacotherapy.
  - Manage drug–drug interaction alerts and resolve discrepancies.
  - Provide medication counseling to patients and staff.
  - Monitor therapeutic drug levels and adjust dosing regimens.
- **High-value decision points:**
  - Whether to override or act on a drug–drug interaction alert.
  - Selecting alternative therapy when first-line options are contraindicated.
  - Determining dose adjustments for renal/hepatic impairment.
  - Prioritizing which medication reconciliation discrepancies to resolve first.
- **Available information and cues:** Patient medication history, lab values (renal/hepatic function), EHR alerts, clinical guidelines, drug interaction databases, prescriber notes. [bmjopen.bmj](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/13/12/e075512)
- **Plausible alternatives:** Accept alert and change therapy, contact prescriber for clarification, monitor without change, document rationale for override, escalate to pharmacy supervisor.
- **Constraints and pressures:** Time pressure from urgent orders, high consequence of error (patient harm), alert fatigue, conflicting stakeholder demands (prescriber preferences vs. guidelines), regulatory documentation requirements.
- **Consequences of error:** Patient harm (adverse drug events), legal/regulatory exposure, reputational damage, increased length of stay, financial costs.
- **Primary scenario archetype:** A hospital pharmacist notices a discrepancy between a medication reconciliation record and an urgent prescription while the prescribing physician is unavailable during night shift.
- **Alternative scenario archetype 1:** During interdisciplinary rounds, a pharmacist must recommend an alternative antibiotic for a septic patient with multiple allergies and renal impairment while the team is under time pressure.
- **Alternative scenario archetype 2:** A pharmacist receives a high-severity drug–drug interaction alert for a patient on 15+ medications and must decide whether to override, modify, or escalate.
- **Natural CTA interview structure:** Critical Decision Method (CDM) focusing on drug–drug interaction management episode with probes for cues, alternatives, and decision requirements. [bmjopen.bmj](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/13/12/e075512)
- **Vocabulary-control suitability:** HIGH (standardized medication and interaction terminology)
- **Ambiguity-control suitability:** HIGH (clear guidelines, but interpretation varies)
- **Counterfactual suitability:** HIGH (clear alternative decisions with different outcomes)
- **Country or regulatory dependence:** REGIONALLY_ADAPTABLE (scope varies by jurisdiction, but core decision-making is transferable)
- **Potentially natural biases:** Anchoring Bias (first diagnosis/treatment anchors subsequent decisions), Confirmation Bias (seeking evidence supporting initial therapy), Omission Bias (preferring inaction over action with risk), Action Bias (intervening when monitoring is safer), Overconfidence Bias, Availability Bias (recent adverse event influences judgment), Framing Effect (how alert is presented), Default Bias (accepting EHR defaults), Base-Rate Neglect (ignoring interaction prevalence). [bmjopen.bmj](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/13/12/e075512)
- **Biases requiring caution:** Gambler's Fallacy (unlikely to fit naturally), Bandwagon Effect (requires careful design), Decoy Effect (requires artificial choice architecture).
- **Biases likely to be unsuitable:** Representativeness (limited applicability), Primacy/Recency Bias (less central to medication decisions), Risk Tolerance/Aversion (better captured via other biases).

### Occupation H02: Registered Nurse (ICU)

- **Standard title:** Registered Nurse (Intensive Care Unit)
- **Role classification:** Recognized occupation
- **Domain fit:** Frontline monitoring, anomaly detection, and emergency response in high-reliability critical care. [ovid](https://www.ovid.com/journals/jenu/fulltext/10.1016/j.jen.2025.05.013~a-cognitive-task-analysis-for-developing-a-clinical-decision)
- **Typical work setting:** Hospital ICU, high-acuity inpatient units
- **Expertise level:** Independent practitioner (BSN + ICU experience/certification)
- **Main stakeholders:** Intensivists, patients, families, respiratory therapists, other nurses, charge nurse
- **Core tasks:**
  - Continuous monitoring of vital signs, hemodynamics, and ventilator settings.
  - Detecting early signs of deterioration (sepsis, arrhythmias, respiratory failure).
  - Administering medications and titrating infusions per protocol.
  - Coordinating with multidisciplinary team during emergencies.
  - Documenting patient status and communicating changes.
- **High-value decision points:**
  - Recognizing subtle signs of deterioration before overt crisis.
  - Deciding when to escalate to physician vs. manage independently.
  - Prioritizing interventions during simultaneous patient needs.
  - Interpreting conflicting monitor alarms and clinical signs.
- **Available information and cues:** Vital sign trends, monitor waveforms, ventilator data, lab results, patient appearance/behavior, family reports, EHR alerts. [ovid](https://www.ovid.com/journals/jenu/fulltext/10.1016/j.jen.2025.05.013~a-cognitive-task-analysis-for-developing-a-clinical-decision)
- **Plausible alternatives:** Escalate immediately, continue monitoring, adjust interventions, call rapid response, delegate to another nurse.
- **Constraints and pressures:** High time pressure, interruption-heavy, high consequence of error, resource constraints (staffing ratios), emotionally charged environment.
- **Consequences of error:** Patient harm or death, operational disruption, legal/regulatory exposure, reputational damage, moral distress.
- **Primary scenario archetype:** An ICU nurse notices subtle changes in a post-operative patient's respiratory pattern and oxygen saturation trend while managing two other unstable patients and receiving frequent interruptions.
- **Alternative scenario archetype 1:** During a code blue, the nurse must prioritize medication administration, defibrillation timing, and communication while the team leader is inexperienced.
- **Alternative scenario archetype 2:** A nurse detects a discrepancy between a ventilator alarm and the patient's clinical appearance, requiring troubleshooting before calling respiratory therapy.
- **Natural CTA interview structure:** CDM with focus on anomaly detection and escalation decisions under time pressure. [ovid](https://www.ovid.com/journals/jenu/fulltext/10.1016/j.jen.2025.05.013~a-cognitive-task-analysis-for-developing-a-clinical-decision)
- **Vocabulary-control suitability:** MEDIUM (clinical terminology standardized, but interpretation varies)
- **Ambiguity-control suitability:** HIGH (incomplete information is inherent)
- **Counterfactual suitability:** HIGH (clear alternative actions with different outcomes)
- **Country or regulatory dependence:** BROADLY_TRANSFERABLE (core nursing decisions are similar across jurisdictions)
- **Potentially natural biases:** Premature Closure (stopping assessment after initial finding), Availability Bias (recent event influences judgment), Overconfidence Bias, Confirmation Bias (seeking evidence supporting initial impression), Anchoring Bias (first vital sign anchors interpretation), Omission Bias (delaying escalation), Action Bias (intervening unnecessarily), Recency Bias (most recent patient influences judgment), Affect Heuristic (emotional state influences risk perception). [ovid](https://www.ovid.com/journals/jenu/fulltext/10.1016/j.jen.2025.05.013~a-cognitive-task-analysis-for-developing-a-clinical-decision)
- **Biases requiring caution:** Base-Rate Neglect (requires careful design), Optimism Bias (requires specific context), Sunk Costs Bias (less natural in acute care).
- **Biases likely to be unsuitable:** Decoy Effect, Default Bias, Bandwagon Effect, Gambler's Fallacy, Framing Effect (less central to monitoring decisions).

### Occupation H03: Radiologic Technologist (MRI/CT)

- **Standard title:** Radiologic Technologist (MRI/CT Specialist)
- **Role classification:** Recognized occupation with specialization
- **Domain fit:** Technical imaging decisions with safety and diagnostic quality implications. [healthcareers](https://healthcareers.app/blog/hospital-occupations-list-50-careers-you-can-pursue-in-a-hospital-setting)
- **Typical work setting:** Hospital radiology department, outpatient imaging center
- **Expertise level:** Independent practitioner (certification + modality-specific training)
- **Main stakeholders:** Radiologists, patients, referring physicians, nurses, IT/PACS staff
- **Core tasks:**
  - Positioning patients and selecting imaging protocols.
  - Adjusting technical parameters for image quality and safety.
  - Screening for contraindications (metallic implants, contrast allergies).
  - Troubleshooting equipment malfunctions.
  - Ensuring radiation safety and dose optimization.
- **High-value decision points:**
  - Whether to proceed with scan given patient safety concerns.
  - Selecting protocol modifications for complex cases.
  - Deciding when to repeat scans vs. accept suboptimal images.
  - Escalating equipment issues vs. continuing with workarounds.
- **Available information and cues:** Patient history, screening forms, prior imaging, protocol guidelines, equipment status indicators, radiation dose metrics.
- **Plausible alternatives:** Proceed with scan, modify protocol, delay for additional screening, consult radiologist, cancel/reschedule.
- **Constraints and pressures:** Moderate time pressure, safety-critical, regulatory compliance (radiation dose), patient anxiety, equipment availability.
- **Consequences of error:** Patient harm (radiation exposure, contrast reactions), diagnostic delays, operational disruption, legal/regulatory exposure.
- **Primary scenario archetype:** A CT technologist screens a patient with unclear history of metallic implants and must decide whether to proceed with a time-sensitive scan while the radiologist is unavailable.
- **Alternative scenario archetype 1:** During a complex MRI, the technologist must adjust protocol parameters mid-scan due to patient movement while balancing image quality and scan time.
- **Alternative scenario archetype 2:** A technologist notices inconsistent radiation dose readings and must decide whether to halt the scanner, troubleshoot, or continue with modified protocols.
- **Natural CTA interview structure:** CDM focusing on safety screening and protocol selection decisions.
- **Vocabulary-control suitability:** HIGH (standardized protocols and terminology)
- **Ambiguity-control suitability:** MEDIUM (some ambiguity in patient history)
- **Counterfactual suitability:** MEDIUM (clear alternatives, but outcomes less varied)
- **Country or regulatory dependence:** REGIONALLY_ADAPTABLE (radiation safety regulations vary, but core decisions transferable)
- **Potentially natural biases:** Omission Bias (preferring not to act on uncertain safety concerns), Action Bias (proceeding despite uncertainty), Anchoring Bias (first screening response anchors decision), Availability Bias (recent adverse event influences judgment), Overconfidence Bias, Default Bias (accepting default protocols).
- **Biases requiring caution:** Confirmation Bias (requires careful design), Framing Effect (how safety question is asked), Loss/Gain Framing (requires specific context).
- **Biases likely to be unsuitable:** Premature Closure, Base-Rate Neglect, Gambler's Fallacy, Bandwagon Effect, Decoy Effect, Representativeness.

### Occupation H04: Medical Laboratory Scientist (Clinical Chemistry)

- **Standard title:** Medical Laboratory Scientist (Clinical Chemistry)
- **Role classification:** Recognized occupation
- **Domain fit:** Diagnostic testing decisions with direct impact on patient diagnosis and treatment. [healthcareers](https://healthcareers.app/blog/hospital-occupations-list-50-careers-you-can-pursue-in-a-hospital-setting)
- **Typical work setting:** Hospital or reference laboratory, clinical chemistry section
- **Expertise level:** Independent practitioner (BS/MS + certification)
- **Main stakeholders:** Pathologists, clinicians, laboratory technicians, quality assurance staff
- **Core tasks:**
  - Running and validating clinical chemistry assays.
  - Detecting and troubleshooting anomalous results.
  - Determining when to repeat tests vs. report results.
  - Interpreting quality control data and flagging out-of-range values.
  - Communicating critical results to clinicians.
- **High-value decision points:**
  - Whether to report an anomalous result or repeat the test.
  - Identifying instrument malfunction vs. true patient abnormality.
  - Deciding when to escalate to pathologist or supervisor.
  - Prioritizing which critical results to communicate first.
- **Available information and cues:** Assay results, quality control charts, instrument flags, patient history, delta checks, prior results, reagent lot information.
- **Plausible alternatives:** Report result, repeat test, dilute and re-run, escalate to pathologist, hold result for review.
- **Constraints and pressures:** Moderate time pressure, high consequence of error (misdiagnosis), regulatory compliance (CLIA/CAP), resource constraints (reagent availability), documentation requirements.
- **Consequences of error:** Misdiagnosis, delayed treatment, patient harm, legal/regulatory exposure, operational disruption.
- **Primary scenario archetype:** A laboratory scientist detects a critically abnormal potassium result that conflicts with the patient's clinical picture and must decide whether to report, repeat, or escalate while the stat turnaround time is expiring.
- **Alternative scenario archetype 1:** During a high-volume shift, the scientist must prioritize which critical results to communicate first while managing multiple instrument alerts.
- **Alternative scenario archetype 2:** A scientist notices a trend of out-of-range quality control values and must decide whether to halt the analyzer, troubleshoot, or continue with modified protocols.
- **Natural CTA interview structure:** CDM focusing on anomaly detection and result validation decisions.
- **Vocabulary-control suitability:** HIGH (standardized laboratory terminology)
- **Ambiguity-control suitability:** HIGH (conflicting data is inherent)
- **Counterfactual suitability:** HIGH (clear alternative decisions with different outcomes)
- **Country or regulatory dependence:** REGIONALLY_ADAPTABLE (regulatory frameworks vary, but core decisions transferable)
- **Potentially natural biases:** Premature Closure (accepting first result), Confirmation Bias (seeking evidence supporting initial interpretation), Anchoring Bias (prior result anchors judgment), Availability Bias (recent error influences decision), Overconfidence Bias, Omission Bias (delaying reporting), Action Bias (repeating unnecessarily), Base-Rate Neglect (ignoring prevalence of spurious results).
- **Biases requiring caution:** Framing Effect (how result is presented), Loss/Gain Framing (requires specific context), Optimism Bias (requires careful design).
- **Biases likely to be unsuitable:** Gambler's Fallacy, Bandwagon Effect, Decoy Effect, Default Bias, Representativeness, Sunk Costs Bias.

### Occupation H05: Physical Therapist (Outpatient Orthopedics)

- **Standard title:** Physical Therapist (Outpatient Orthopedics)
- **Role classification:** Recognized occupation with specialization
- **Domain fit:** Clinical judgment in rehabilitation planning with patient-specific adaptations. [shawnee](https://www.shawnee.edu/blog/clinical-administrative-careers-in-health-science)
- **Typical work setting:** Outpatient clinic, private practice, rehabilitation center
- **Expertise level:** Independent practitioner (DPT + orthopedic specialization)
- **Main stakeholders:** Patients, referring physicians, insurance payers, PT assistants, family members
- **Core tasks:**
  - Assessing patient movement patterns and functional limitations.
  - Designing individualized treatment plans.
  - Modifying exercises based on patient response.
  - Determining progression vs. regression of interventions.
  - Documenting outcomes for insurance authorization.
- **High-value decision points:**
  - Selecting initial treatment approach from multiple options.
  - Deciding when to progress, maintain, or regress interventions.
  - Determining when to refer back to physician vs. continue therapy.
  - Balancing patient preferences with evidence-based protocols.
- **Available information and cues:** Patient history, imaging reports, physical exam findings, patient-reported outcomes, insurance guidelines, prior therapy notes.
- **Plausible alternatives:** Continue current plan, modify exercises, refer to physician, discharge patient, escalate to specialist.
- **Constraints and pressures:** Moderate time pressure, resource constraints (visit limits), reputational pressure (patient satisfaction), conflicting stakeholder demands (patient goals vs. insurance requirements), documentation burden.
- **Consequences of error:** Suboptimal recovery, patient dissatisfaction, financial loss (denied claims), reputational damage, delayed return to function.
- **Primary scenario archetype:** An outpatient PT must decide whether to progress a post-surgical knee patient to weight-bearing exercises despite the patient reporting increased pain, while insurance visit limits are approaching.
- **Alternative scenario archetype 1:** A PT notices conflicting information between the surgeon's protocol and the patient's functional capacity and must decide which to prioritize.
- **Alternative scenario archetype 2:** During a busy clinic day, the PT must prioritize which patients to see first when two patients arrive late and one has acute pain.
- **Natural CTA interview structure:** CDM focusing on treatment planning and progression decisions.
- **Vocabulary-control suitability:** MEDIUM (clinical terminology standardized, but patient communication varies)
- **Ambiguity-control suitability:** MEDIUM (patient-reported outcomes are subjective)
- **Counterfactual suitability:** MEDIUM (clear alternatives, but outcomes less immediate)
- **Country or regulatory dependence:** REGIONALLY_ADAPTABLE (scope of practice varies, but core decisions transferable)
- **Potentially natural biases:** Anchoring Bias (first assessment anchors treatment), Confirmation Bias (seeking evidence supporting initial plan), Overconfidence Bias, Optimism Bias (overestimating patient progress), Sunk Costs Bias (continuing ineffective treatment due to time invested), Framing Effect (how patient describes pain), Loss/Gain Framing (emphasizing recovery vs. avoiding setbacks), Default Bias (following standard protocols).
- **Biases requiring caution:** Availability Bias (requires specific context), Premature Closure (less natural in iterative therapy), Action Bias (requires careful design).
- **Biases likely to be unsuitable:** Gambler's Fallacy, Bandwagon Effect, Decoy Effect, Base-Rate Neglect, Representativeness, Omission Bias.

### Occupation H08: Public Health Epidemiologist

- **Standard title:** Public Health Epidemiologist
- **Role classification:** Recognized occupation
- **Domain fit:** Forecasting, risk assessment, and planning decisions with population health implications. [shawnee](https://www.shawnee.edu/blog/clinical-administrative-careers-in-health-science)
- **Typical work setting:** Public health department, government agency, academic institution, field investigations
- **Expertise level:** Specialist/expert (MPH/PhD + experience)
- **Main stakeholders:** Public health officials, healthcare providers, community organizations, media, general public
- **Core tasks:**
  - Analyzing disease surveillance data and identifying outbreaks.
  - Designing and evaluating public health interventions.
  - Communicating risk to stakeholders and the public.
  - Coordinating multi-agency response efforts.
  - Preparing reports and policy recommendations.
- **High-value decision points:**
  - Determining whether a cluster of cases constitutes an outbreak.
  - Selecting intervention strategies from multiple options.
  - Deciding when to escalate to emergency response.
  - Balancing transparency with public panic in communications.
- **Available information and cues:** Surveillance data, lab reports, field investigation findings, historical trends, stakeholder input, media reports, policy guidelines.
- **Plausible alternatives:** Declare outbreak, continue monitoring, escalate response, modify intervention, communicate differently.
- **Constraints and pressures:** Moderate to high time pressure, high consequence of error (public health impact), political/reputational pressure, conflicting stakeholder demands, incomplete/ambiguous data.
- **Consequences of error:** Public health impact (disease spread), political/reputational damage, operational disruption, financial costs, loss of public trust.
- **Primary scenario archetype:** An epidemiologist detects an unusual cluster of respiratory illness cases and must decide whether to declare an outbreak while data is incomplete and media attention is increasing.
- **Alternative scenario archetype 1:** During a multi-state outbreak, the epidemiologist must prioritize which intervention strategies to recommend while resources are limited and stakeholders have conflicting preferences.
- **Alternative scenario archetype 2:** An epidemiologist must decide how to communicate risk to the public when preliminary data suggests a potential threat but confirmation is pending.
- **Natural CTA interview structure:** CDM focusing on outbreak detection and intervention decisions.
- **Vocabulary-control suitability:** HIGH (standardized epidemiological terminology)
- **Ambiguity-control suitability:** HIGH (incomplete data is inherent)
- **Counterfactual suitability:** HIGH (clear alternative decisions with different outcomes)
- **Country or regulatory dependence:** REGIONALLY_ADAPTABLE (public health systems vary, but core decisions transferable)

**Near-duplicate occupations retained and why:**
- **H02 (ICU RN) and H09 (Respiratory Therapist):** Both work in high-acuity settings but differ in decision authority, information access, and primary interaction patterns. RN focuses on overall patient monitoring and escalation; RT focuses on respiratory-specific troubleshooting and ventilation management.
- **H01 (Clinical Pharmacist) and H04 (Medical Laboratory Scientist):** Both involve diagnostic support but differ in information environment (medication vs. lab data) and consequence profiles (drug interactions vs. test accuracy).
- **H06 (Health Information Manager) and H10 (Medical Coder):** Both involve compliance but differ in decision authority (managerial vs. individual coding) and stakeholder relationships.

**Overrepresented categories:**
- **Clinical/care-oriented work setting (42%):** Healthcare naturally concentrates in clinical settings, but this is balanced by technical, administrative, and hybrid roles.
- **Independent practitioner expertise level (83%):** Healthcare decision-making is predominantly at the independent practitioner level; senior/expert roles are less common but represented (H06, H08).
- **Health/welfare consequence profile (58%):** Inherent to healthcare domain; balanced by safety, legal, financial, and operational consequences.

**Underrepresented categories:**
- **Senior/manager and specialist/expert expertise levels (8% each):** Only H06 and H08 represent these levels. Consider adding Hospital Administrator or Chief Medical Officer if more senior roles are needed.
- **Individual work with tools/systems interaction pattern (33%):** Could be increased with more technical roles (e.g., PACS Administrator, Laboratory Informatics Specialist).
- **Financial consequence profile (25%):** Could be increased with Revenue Cycle Manager or Healthcare Finance roles.

**Recommended substitutions, if needed:**
- If more senior roles are needed: Replace H03 (Radiologic Technologist) with **Radiology Department Manager** or H10 (Medical Coder) with **Revenue Cycle Manager**.

**Overall balance assessment:**
The roster achieves good coverage across work settings, decision types, information environments, and consequence profiles. The concentration in clinical settings and independent practitioner levels is natural for healthcare and is balanced by technical, administrative, and hybrid roles. Bias compatibility is high across most occupations, with clear decision episodes and meaningful consequences. Jurisdictional dependence is minimized, with most roles marked as broadly transferable or regionally adaptable.
