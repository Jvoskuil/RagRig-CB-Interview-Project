Interviewer: Moving to the next stage — figuring out the discharge destination itself. What information did you have about the daughter's ability to help? Interviewer: Did you use the discharge risk-scoring tool at that point? Participant: Not right away. Interviewer: What did the fuller chart review and the tool eventually show? Participant: When I did look closer, his actual readmission history was lower than what my gut had been telling me. Interviewer: Let's talk about the final decision — finalizing the plan. What were the options at that point? Interviewer: What did you think that extra day would actually do to him? Participant: Honestly, I pictured him getting more worked up the longer it dragged on — maybe starting to feel like we'd gone back on our word, losing some trust in the plan altogether. Interviewer: What came out of the home safety evaluation once it was finally done? Participant: After discharge, it turned up a loose stair rail and poor lighting in the bathroom. Interviewer: Looking back, if the PT assessment had come in before your first conversation with him, do you think anything would have gone differently? Participant: Maybe. I might have gone in with more questions instead of forming an opinion so quickly. Interviewer: If you hadn't had that other readmission case fresh in mind, would your risk read have been different? Participant: Possibly less urgent-feeling, yeah. It's hard to separate that out completely. Interviewer: Any point where slowing down might have changed the outcome? Participant: Probably the caregiver piece. If I'd pushed harder on specifics with the daughter earlier instead of assuming it would work out, we might have caught the coverage gap sooner. Interviewer: What would you do differently with a similar case tomorrow? Participant: Probably run the risk tool earlier, and ask more direct questions about caregiving capacity right from the first call, rather than filling in the gaps myself.
